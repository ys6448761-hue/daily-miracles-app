Establish an evidence-bounded experiential burden pattern for the physical approach to Hyangiram Hermitage, sufficient to support honest difficulty characterization for H-1 (difficulty description) and suitability judgment for H-2.

## §12 Pacing / Rest Pattern

**Evidence:**
- EI-HY-002-A: "쉬엄쉬엄 여유롭게" (take it slowly with breaks) — standard Korean visitor advice; even at this pace, 20 min
- EI-HY-002-C: Natural rest points at statues area, small cafe, temple grounds — multiple stopping points exist along the route
- EI-HY-002-D: "Take your time and pace yourself" — pacing is explicitly recommended
- EI-HY-002-B: Author completed circuit energetically, suggesting pacing was self-managed

**Pattern:** Visitors naturally rest at intermediate points (statues/gate areas, small cafe) during ascent. Pacing is universally recommended. The route structure (successive distinct gate/passage structures from HY-001) provides natural interruption points that function as rest opportunities.

## §16 Exceptions

1. **Alternative ascent route (평지길 — flat road):** Available for both ascent AND descent (not just descent as HY-001 documented). Gentler slope; EI-HY-002-B confirms it is accessible for ascent. Allows lower-fitness visitors to self-select into easier route. Does NOT eliminate all difficulty — still a walk to the temple.

2. **Rear parking access (절 뒷길 주차장):** A staff/operator-accessible rear parking area exists near the temple (neoplats.com). Accessible via taxi (non-standard). Not a publicly advertised visitor option. This exception dramatically reduces the physical approach for those who can access it. NOT to be presented as a standard visitor option.

3. **Mobility-impaired barriers:** Official KTO accessibility data confirms no elevator, no wheelchair, step/curb at entrance. Visitors with mobility impairments face real structural obstacles. The flat road alternative may partially reduce stair burden but is not confirmed as wheelchair-accessible.

4. **Child capability:** Children as young as 5 can complete the standard hermitage visit with adult accompaniment (two independent English WE sources). This is not a blanket "child-friendly" verdict — the physical demand is real; young children need adult support.

5. **Geomosan summit as distinct harder route:** The fitness caution about poor knee joints and weak cardiopulmonary function (from Hankookilbo) applies to the Geomosan summit section with nearly vertical iron stairs — NOT the standard hermitage approach. Scoping this carefully to avoid misapplication.

## §19 Pattern Synthesis

**A. CORE RECURRING BURDEN:**  
Multi-segment steep staircase ascent producing breathlessness and sweat for a typical adult visitor. The difficulty is steepness-based, not distance-based — the route is relatively short but consistently steep. This pattern is confirmed across Korean and English WE independently.

**B. MAIN PHYSICAL CAUSES:**  
(1) Sustained steep stone staircase with no extended flat recovery; (2) iron stair section toward midpoint/upper section; (3) Haetalmun narrow rock passage requiring posture change (adds effort even if brief); (4) Summer heat as the most documented context multiplier.

**C. BURDEN INTENSITY PATTERN:**  
Moderate-to-demanding for a typical adult. Breathlessness and sweating are normal expected responses (not anomalies). "Considerably steep" is the Korean consensus. Not extreme — multiple healthy adults complete without distress. Intensity is higher than a typical urban stairway; comparable to a brisk hill-walk with irregular steps.

**D. PACING/REST PATTERN:**  
Active pacing is universally recommended. Natural rest points exist at gate structures, statues, small cafe, and temple complex. Visitors who break at these points successfully complete without distress. Continuous non-stop ascent would be more demanding.

**E. FITNESS/TRAVELER VARIATION:**  
High-fitness visitors: manageable, may characterize as "not difficult." Average-fitness visitors: breathless and sweating; completing with effort. Low-fitness or heat-sensitive visitors: each step can be challenging (summer); significantly harder. Mobility-impaired: real structural barriers.

**F. CONTEXT-CONDITIONAL VARIATION:**  
Summer heat is the strongest documented context multiplier. Load-carrying increases exertion. Spring/autumn: standard difficulty pattern. Rain-slippery conditions: not captured but structurally plausible from steep stone steps.

**G. COMPLETION PATTERN:**  
High completion rate for healthy ambulatory adults including families with children. Two route options (stairway vs flat road) allow fitness self-selection. Mobility-impaired visitors face barriers not resolved by existing facilities.

**H. IMPORTANT EXCEPTIONS:**  
(1) Flat road (평지길) available for ASCENT — not just descent; significantly reduces stair burden; (2) Young children (5+) can complete with adult accompaniment; (3) Rear staff parking exists but is not a standard visitor option; (4) Mobility-impaired visitors have real unresolved barriers.

**I. KNOWN LIMITS:**  
- Step count (398) is visitor-averaged, not officially published  
- Korean sources primarily describe spring/non-summer conditions; English sources add summer dimension  
- Rain/slippery conditions not captured  
- Flat road capability for limited mobility (e.g., walker users) not confirmed  
- Senior-specific experiential patterns: HY-001+HY-002 provide general context but HY-003 must independently collect elder-specific accounts
